# Rigged fox: art brief

The figure an agent starts from for a four-legged animal that walks, trots, gallops, jumps or flinches: a dog, a wolf, a fox or a horse. An original red fox seen from the side, facing right. The design is our own; nothing is taken from a game.

- **Canvas and display:** 48×32, origin `[24, 30]` (the anchor under the body), `rig.ground` 28 (the ankle row). The paws' soles sit on row 29. Review at 1× and 6×. A jump with `apex: 3` and the bushy tail at full swing stay inside the canvas.
- **Landmarks:** tall pointed ears with dark backs, a long snout with a black nose, a white cheek and throat, dark stockings on all four legs, and a bushy tail with a cream tip. The gap under the belly keeps the four legs readable in every gait.
- **Construction:** a round `mass` at the hips is the rig body. The chest is a bone standing out in front of it (`width [6, 7]`, deeper at the shoulders than the waist), `rig.chest`, which the gallop bends against the hips; the fore legs hang from its shoulder point and the hind legs from the hips. A short neck bone (`rig.neck`) carries a grid head and turns back against the chest, so the head stays steadier than the body. Each leg is two bones solved to the ground: an orange upper bone and a dark stocking, with a small grid paw. The hind legs rest bent back at the hock. A three-bone tail (`rig.tail`) sways.
- **Joins:** the chest, the neck and each near leg's upper bone start with a `socket` cap and are listed after what they attach to, so they blend into its fill: the fur has no lines through it, and a leg reads where it leaves the body. The tail and the far legs, drawn behind the body, keep their outline where the body meets them.
- **Palette (11 colours):** `k` outline. Fur `R r o y`, stockings `c d e f`, cream `v w`. Light comes from the upper left (225°). Near limbs are one ramp step lighter than far ones (orange `o` over `r`, stocking `e` over `d`), never on the darkest step, which merges with the outline.
- **Motion:** idle (8 frames), walk (12 frames, a lateral sequence with three feet down), trot (diagonal pairs), run (a rotary gallop with an extended and a gathered flight, `flex` at its default 10°), jump and hurt, with speeds, strides and lifts as written in the source. Planted feet are measured by the motion review; the biped gait checks report `null`.
- **Weaknesses:** the legs are short two-bone chains, so a fore leg's elbow and wrist share one joint: it folds back under weight and forward as the foot lifts. The paws are flat grids without roll views. The gathered gallop frames cross all four dark stockings under the belly. The tail sways by template angles, not by speed.
